# Role card: Mechanical

**Your part:** The 3D-printed case

**In one sentence:** It protects the parts and puts each one where it can do its job.

## Reference guide

- Every opening has a job: a vent for the sensor, a window for the screen, holes for the light pipes, an opening for USB-C, a bay for the battery.
- The sensor mounts under the vent slots in the cover, away from the warm board.
- Cables need strain relief, so a tug does not pull a wire out of a terminal.

## Only you know

- In the cover: the sensor vent (five slots), the display window, and two 5 × 5 mm light pipe holes. In the body: the battery bay (the walled-off section), the USB-C opening (8 × 14 mm, top left) and the switch hole (10 mm round, top right).
- The cover closes with four stainless steel M3 socket head cap screws at the corners. The boards mount with nylon M2.5 screws.
- The lid goes on last, after the test passes. Opening it again costs time.
- The battery sits in its bay with its wire leaving toward the charger. Nothing pinched.

## Interfaces

- **You give:** A fixed place for every part.
- **You need:** Sensor at the vent (Sensor). The shape, the window and the light pipe holes from Human Interaction. USB-C port at its opening (Microcontroller + Code). Battery in the bay (Power).

## Your build task

Seat each part in the case, route the cables, check nothing is pinched. Leave the lid off until the test passes, then close the case.

## If something goes wrong

Temperature drifts up after the lid goes on: the sensor is too close to the board or the vent is blocked.

## Check yourself

If the sensor sat next to the microcontroller with no vent, which reading would be wrong, and in which direction?
